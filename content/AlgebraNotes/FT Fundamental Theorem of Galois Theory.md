- Galois group of a quartic: We consider the splitting field of the polynomial $f(x) = x^4+px^2+q$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Note that this quartic has four roots: $$\begin{aligned} x_1 &= \sqrt{\frac{-p+\sqrt{p^2-4q}}{2}} \\ x_2 &= -\sqrt{\frac{-p+\sqrt{p^2-4q}}{2}} \\ x_3 &= \sqrt{\frac{-p-\sqrt{p^2-4q}}{2}} \\ x_4 &= -\sqrt{\frac{-p-\sqrt{p^2-4q}}{2}} \end{aligned}$$ We build the splitting field of $f(x)$ in several stages.
	- Starting from the base field $\mathbb{Q}$, note that any permutation of the roots fixes $\mathbb{Q}$ - we therefore have that $\text{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Q}) \cong S_4$ is the set of all permutations of the 4 roots
	- We next adjoin $p,q$ to $\mathbb{Q}$ to form $\mathbb{Q}(p, q) = F_1$. Note that we have that in $F_1$ that $$\begin{align} x_1 + x_2 = 0 \\ x_3 + x_4 = 0\end{align}$$Not all permutations of the roots keep these relations invariant - these relations however are invariant under the permutations in $\{ e, (12), (34), (12)(34), (13)(24), (14)(23), (1324), (1423) \} \cong D_4 \le S_4$
	- We next have that $x_1^2 - x_3^2 = \sqrt{p^2-4q} \equiv \omega$ is a relation in $\mathbb{Q}(p,q,\omega) = F_1(\omega) = F_2$. Not all permutations above keep this relation invariant - we have that this relation is invariant under the permutations $\{ e, (12), (34), (12)(34) \}\cong K_4 \le D_4$
	- Now, letting $\mu = \sqrt{\frac{-p-\omega}{2}}$, we have a new relation $x_3 - x_4 = 2 \mu$ in the field $F_1(\mu)=F_2$; this relation is invariant under the permutations $\{e, (12)\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$
	- Finally, letting $\eta = \sqrt{\frac{-p+\omega}{2}}$, we have the relation $x_1 - x_2 = 2 \eta$ in the field $F_2(\eta) = F_3$; this relation is invariant under only the identity permutation
	- We therefore have the following *ascending* tower of fields and corresponding *descending* tower of Galois groups: $$\begin{array}{ccccc}\mathbb{Q}  &\subseteq &\mathbb{Q}(p,q) = F_1 &\subseteq &F_1\left(\sqrt{p^2-4q}\right) = F_2 &\subseteq &F_2(x_3)=F_3 &\subseteq &F_3(x_1) = F_4\\ S_4&\subseteq&D_4&\subseteq&K&\subseteq&\mathbb{Z}_2&\subseteq&\{1\}\end{array}$$
